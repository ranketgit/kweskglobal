You need to furnish your offices and you're torn between a standard **office chair** and an executive armchair? This choice, often underestimated, directly impacts your employees' comfort, health and productivity. According to occupational health studies, musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs) account for 87% of recognized occupational diseases in France, and a poor seat is often the trigger. Understanding the **difference between office chairs and armchairs** will help you invest wisely and give your teams the seating they deserve.

## Office Chair: Definition and Characteristics

Before comparing, it's essential to clearly define each type of seating. The confusion between a standard office chair and an executive office armchair is common, as both serve the same purpose: working while seated. Yet their design differs fundamentally.

### The Office Chair: Compact and Functional

The office chair is characterized by:

- **A minimalist design**: no armrests or headrest in most models
- **A reduced footprint**: ideal for small spaces and open-plan offices
- **Lightweight construction**: easy to move from one workstation to another
- **An accessible price**: generally between €100 and €400

The office chair is particularly suited for occasional use or shared spaces where flexibility comes first. It fits easily into an [open space layout](https://kwesk.com/en/blog/amenagement-bureau/comment-amenager-open-space) without visually overloading the space.

### The Executive Armchair: Advanced Comfort and Ergonomics

The **executive office armchair** offers a completely different level of comfort:

- **A high backrest** with integrated lumbar support
- **Adjustable armrests** (height, depth and orientation)
- **Dense padding** for prolonged support
- **Tilt mechanisms** to vary positions
- **A headrest** on premium models

The executive armchair is designed for professionals who spend 6 to 8 hours a day seated. It's the preferred choice for management positions, developers or any sedentary occupation.

## Office Chair vs Armchair: The Complete Comparison

To fully grasp the difference between an office chair and an armchair, here's a detailed comparison on the criteria that truly matter in a professional environment.

### Ergonomics and Body Support

This is the most significant difference. An ergonomic armchair reduces the risk of MSDs by 35 to 45% compared to standard seating (Ergonomics Journal, 2025). It follows the natural curves of the spine thanks to its adjustable lumbar support.

The office chair, even a good quality one, offers more limited support. Without armrests, your forearms aren't supported, generating tension in the shoulders and neck over time.

### Daily Comfort

Comfort is measured over time. An office chair may seem comfortable for the first few hours, but the lack of support becomes noticeable by end of day. The **executive armchair**, with its multiple adjustments, maintains consistent comfort throughout.

Studies show that employees suffering from moderate lower back pain display a 15 to 25% decrease in productivity compared to pain-free colleagues. A suitable armchair prevents this scenario.

## How to Choose Between an Office Chair and an Armchair?

The choice doesn't come down to simply "better or worse." It depends on concrete criteria related to your professional usage.

### Criterion 1: Daily Usage Duration

- **Less than 4 hours per day**: an ergonomic office chair is perfectly sufficient
- **4 to 6 hours per day**: an armchair with lumbar support is recommended
- **More than 6 hours per day**: a premium ergonomic armchair becomes essential

Research shows that investing in ergonomic furniture can reduce MSD incidence by up to 60% in an office environment. For sedentary jobs, pairing a good armchair with a [sit-stand desk](https://kwesk.com/en/blog/ergonomie/bureau-assis-debout) is the ideal combination.

### Criterion 2: User Profiles

Each position has its needs:

- **Reception and meeting rooms**: comfortable and stackable chairs
- **Administrative positions**: armchairs with lumbar support and armrests
- **Management and executives**: premium armchairs with leather and headrest (discover our guide to [leather office armchairs](https://kwesk.com/en/blog/mobilier-bureau/fauteuil-de-bureau-cuir-guide-luxe-dirigeants))
- **Call centers and intensive use**: reinforced armchairs certified for 24/7 use

### Criterion 3: Overall Budget

Don't think solely in terms of purchase cost. MSD-related absenteeism costs an average of €4,500 per employee per year. Every euro invested in ergonomics generates between €3 and €6 in savings on health costs and productivity gains.

**Tip**: choose a professional [office chair manufacturer](https://kwesk.com/en) that offers solid warranties rather than a generic furniture distributor.

### Criterion 4: Essential Adjustments

Whether you opt for an office chair or an armchair, check these adjustments:

1. **Seat height**: your feet should be flat on the floor, thighs parallel to the ground
2. **Seat depth**: 2 to 3 fingers of space between the seat edge and the back of your knees
3. **Lumbar support**: adjustable in height and depth to follow your natural curve
4. **Armrests**: adjustable to relieve shoulders (present on armchairs)
5. **Backrest tilt**: to vary positions throughout the day

## When to Choose an Office Chair?

The office chair remains relevant in several professional situations:

- **Coworking spaces** with frequent workstation rotation
- **Meeting rooms** where you sit for less than 2 hours
- **Secondary workstations** used occasionally
- **Tight budgets** for initial equipment (provided you plan an upgrade later)

Some modern office chairs now integrate ergonomic features such as a curved backrest or tilt mechanism. It's a good compromise when space or budget is limited. You can also explore [office chairs without wheels](https://kwesk.com/en/blog/mobilier-bureau/chaise-de-bureau-sans-roulettes) for specific environments.

## When to Choose an Executive Armchair?

The **executive armchair** is the clear choice in these contexts:

- **Fixed workstations** occupied more than 4 hours per day
- **Employees suffering from back pain** (see our guide on [office chairs for back pain](https://kwesk.com/en/blog/mobilier-bureau/fauteuil-bureau-contre-le-mal-de-dos))
- **Companies concerned with employer branding**: a good armchair sends a strong signal to your teams
- **Remote work**: a well-equipped [home office](https://kwesk.com/en/blog/amenagement-bureau/bureau-a-la-maison-guide-amenagement-teletravail) boosts productivity by 17 to 22%

Recent studies confirm that an ergonomic armchair improves productivity by 17.5% on average and reduces absenteeism by 15 to 25%. For a workforce of 50 people, that represents hundreds of work hours recovered each year.
