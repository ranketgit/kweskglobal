## What Is a Sit-Stand Desk?

A **sit-stand desk**, also known as a **standing desk** or **height-adjustable desk**, is a workstation whose surface can be adjusted in height. It allows the user to alternate between a seated and standing position throughout the day without interrupting their work.

### How Does a Height-Adjustable Desk Work?

The adjustment mechanism can be:

- **Electric**: one or two motors adjust the height at the push of a button. This is the most popular solution in business settings thanks to its ease of use.
- **Manual crank**: a mechanical system raises or lowers the surface. More affordable, but less practical for frequent changes.
- **Pneumatic**: a gas cylinder handles the adjustment. Fast and quiet, but with a more limited load capacity.

High-end electric models offer advanced features such as **programmable height presets**, allowing you to switch between positions with a single gesture.

## Health Benefits of the Sit-Stand Desk

Sedentary work poses a major public health challenge. Musculoskeletal disorders affect the vast majority of office workers today, and prolonged sitting is directly linked to obesity, cardiovascular disease, and diabetes. The ergonomic **sit-stand desk** provides concrete answers to these issues.

### Reduction of Back and Neck Pain

Sitting for hours places constant pressure on the intervertebral discs. By alternating with a standing position, you reduce this pressure and engage different muscle groups. Studies show that sit-stand desk users report a significant decrease in neck and shoulder pain (Source: PMC, National Library of Medicine).

If your employees already suffer from back pain, pairing a **standing desk** with a [suitable ergonomic chair](https://kwesk.com/en/blog/mobilier-bureau/fauteuil-bureau-contre-le-mal-de-dos) maximizes the benefits for the spine.

### Improved Blood Circulation

Alternating between sitting and standing positions allows blood to circulate more effectively, reducing the risks associated with prolonged inactivity by 40% according to the French National Institute for Research and Safety (INRS). This improved circulation prevents heavy leg sensations and reduces the risk of deep vein thrombosis.

### Boosted Energy Levels and Mood

Working while standing stimulates endorphin production and increases overall energy levels. **Standing desk** users frequently report increased vitality and better mood throughout the day, translating into stronger professional engagement.

## Sit-Stand Desks and Productivity: What Do the Studies Say?

The impact of **height-adjustable desks** on productivity has been the subject of extensive research. Results are broadly positive, though some nuances deserve attention.

### Encouraging Numbers

A study conducted in a call center found that employees using sit-stand desks were approximately **45% more productive** than their seated colleagues, with improvement growing from 23% in the first month to 53% after six months of use (Source: IIE Transactions on Occupational Ergonomics and Human Factors).

Additionally, 65% of people equipped with a height-adjustable desk report better productivity and improved concentration ability after one year of use (Source: Steelcase Research).

### Less Sitting, More Dynamism

A systematic review of available studies shows that sit-stand desks reduce total time spent sitting by **70 to 88 minutes per day** on average. This time recovered in a standing position activates metabolism and keeps the mind alert.

### An Investment That Pays for Itself

For businesses, the calculation is straightforward: less absenteeism related to musculoskeletal disorders, better team concentration, and increased engagement. When you consider that musculoskeletal disorders represent the leading cause of sick leave, investing in an ergonomic **height-adjustable desk** makes perfect sense.

## How to Choose the Best Sit-Stand Desk: Essential Criteria

Choosing a **sit-stand desk** suited to your professional needs relies on several technical criteria that should be carefully evaluated.

### Height Adjustment Range

This is the most important criterion. The desk must be able to adapt to each user's height, in both seated and standing positions. The ideal range is **between 63 and 120 cm**. Beware of entry-level models that max out at 110-115 cm: a tall user will end up leaning forward, which negates the ergonomic benefits.

**Golden rule**: in your working position, your elbows should form a 90° angle with your forearms parallel to the floor, whether you are sitting or standing.

### Desktop Dimensions

The desktop size should match your use:

- **Single monitor setup**: 120 × 60 cm minimum
- **Dual monitor setup**: 140 × 70 cm recommended
- **Full workstation** (monitors + documents): 160 × 80 cm ideal

Depth is often overlooked. Allow **between 60 and 80 cm** to maintain proper distance from your screen and ensure a comfortable workspace.

### Load Capacity

Weigh your complete setup: monitor(s), monitor arm, keyboard, computer, accessories. A quality **height-adjustable desk** supports between 70 and 120 kg. Dual-motor models generally offer better load capacity and increased stability.

### Stability at Maximum Height

A desk that wobbles when you type at the keyboard in standing position is unusable. Test stability at maximum height. Models with a steel frame and lateral crossbars offer the best rigidity.

### Motor Noise Level

In an open-plan office, a noisy motor quickly becomes a problem. Choose models whose noise level stays **below 50 dB**, equivalent to a quiet conversation.

### Additional Features

- **Height presets**: save your favorite positions for instant adjustment
- **Anti-collision system**: the desk stops automatically if it encounters an obstacle
- **Integrated cable management**: for a tidy workspace
- **Position alert**: programmable reminder to encourage you to change positions

## Proper Sit-Stand Desk Positioning: Ergonomic Rules

Buying a **standing desk** is not enough. To reap all the benefits, you need to use it correctly.

### Ideal Seated Posture

- Feet flat on the floor
- Knees bent at 90°
- Back straight, supported by the backrest of your [ergonomic chair](https://kwesk.com/en/blog/mobilier-bureau/siege-ergonomique-bureau-comparatif)
- Shoulders relaxed, elbows at 90°
- Screen at eye level, approximately 50-70 cm from your face

### Ideal Standing Posture

- Weight evenly distributed on both feet
- Knees slightly bent (never locked)
- Spine straight and natural
- Same arm and screen positioning as when seated

### The Right Alternation Rhythm

Ergonomists recommend following the **30-30 rule**: 30 minutes seated, 30 minutes standing. Some experts recommend a 2:1 ratio (40 minutes seated, 20 minutes standing) for beginners. The key is to **move regularly** rather than staying frozen in one position.

## Practical Tips for a Successful Transition

Switching to a **sit-stand desk** requires an adjustment period. Here are our recommendations for a successful transition.

1. **Start gradually**: begin with 15 to 20 minutes standing per hour, then increase progressively. Pushing too hard from the start can cause foot and lower back pain.

2. **Invest in an anti-fatigue mat**: an ergonomic mat reduces pressure on joints and encourages micro-movements of the feet, improving blood circulation.

3. **Wear appropriate footwear**: avoid high heels and overly flat shoes. Choose footwear with good cushioning and arch support.

4. **Organize your workstation**: tidy cables, accessories within reach, properly positioned screen. A [well-arranged computer desk](https://kwesk.com/en/blog/mobilier-bureau/bureau-informatique-meuble-parfait-setup) makes transitions between positions easier.

5. **Listen to your body**: if you feel discomfort, change positions. The goal is not to stand as long as possible but to alternate regularly.
